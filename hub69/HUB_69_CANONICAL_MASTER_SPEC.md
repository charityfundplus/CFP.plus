## 1. Canonical principle

**ONE MASTER → CONTROLLED REPLICATION → SYNCHRONIZED PRESENTATIONS**

HUB 69 is the master coordination architecture. Content must have one canonical logical identity and one designated primary record. Other systems are controlled representations, not competing canonical copies.

## 4. AI operating model

AI Coordinator coordinates work. AI Content, GitHub, Web, Google, Notion, Documents and Review operate in defined lanes. AI must not silently resolve conflicts or invent canonical status.

Every artifact should carry, where applicable:

`Canonical ID → Source Role → Version → Status → Published Targets`

Recommended statuses: `DRAFT`, `REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `PUBLISHED`, `ARCHIVED`.

## 5. Governance gates

Planning approval does not authorize migration. Migration requires a separate Human Governance execution order.

Before migration or publication:

1. Identify canonical source.
2. Verify evidence and permissions.
3. Resolve conflicts.
4. Record mapping and target.
5. Obtain Human Governance approval.
6. Execute only the authorized scope.
7. Review and preserve rollback/reference information.

## 6. Preservation rules

Until explicitly authorized:

- Do not delete legacy content.
- Do not unpublish existing Sites.
- Do not change existing domains.
- Do not change Homepage assignments.
- Do not bulk duplicate or move content.
- Do not overwrite uncertain canonical sources.
- Mark insufficient evidence as `REVIEW` or `UNKNOWN`.

## 7. Synchronization model

The goal is not uncontrolled copying. The goal is controlled replication from an approved master record into designated presentations and technical artifacts.

A synchronization task must identify:

- source record;
- destination;
- version;
- transformation/presentation rules;
- owner/AI lane;
- approval state;
- review date.
