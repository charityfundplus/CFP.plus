## 1. Objective

CFP+ proposes a unified knowledge architecture using **ten chapters numbered 0–9** as the stable global framework for organizing all fields, countries, periods, institutions, technologies, and future developments.

The architecture is intended to:

- provide comprehensive global coverage;
- remain stable without adding Chapter 10 or Chapter 11;
- support unlimited recursive expansion;
- prevent duplicate Canonical Sources;
- remain understandable to both people and AI;
- support Canonical First, Reference First, and Evidence First.

## 3. Universal Coverage Principle

The ten chapters are proposed as the complete top-level classification framework for CFP+.

Every current or future subject must be assigned to one of the ten chapters according to its primary Canonical meaning.

The architecture does not create Chapter 10, Chapter 11, or another parallel top-level chapter. New fields are incorporated through recursive expansion within Chapters 0–9.

This principle is a Review Candidate and requires independent validation before becoming a locked Foundation Rule.

---

## 4. Recursive Expansion Rule

Any chapter or subject node may expand using the same ten-part structure:

- `.0` — Foundation or Overview
- `.1` — Sự Sống
- `.2` — Con Người
- `.3` — Ba Quỹ
- `.4` — Tôn Giáo
- `.5` — Cộng Đồng
- `.6` — AI & Công Nghệ
- `.7` — Doanh Nghiệp
- `.8` — Tổ Chức
- `.9` — Quốc Gia

The same rule may continue at every deeper level.

Examples of conceptual paths:

- `5.6` — AI & Công Nghệ within Cộng Đồng
- `6.1` — Sự Sống within AI & Công Nghệ
- `9.2` — Con Người within Quốc Gia

The separators shown above are presentation aids only. Final Canonical ID formatting remains subject to the CFP+ numeric ID rules and Human Governance decisions.

---

## 5. Primary Chapter Principle

Each subject must have exactly one **Primary Chapter**.

When a subject relates to multiple chapters:

1. Human Governance or the authorized classification process assigns one Primary Chapter.
2. The Primary Chapter contains the Canonical Source.
3. Other chapters reference the Canonical Source.
4. Parallel Canonical copies are prohibited.

Example classification question:

- AI in healthcare may be primarily classified under Chapter 6 or Chapter 1, depending on its Canonical meaning and the Governance decision.
- Once the Primary Chapter is selected, the other chapter must reference it rather than duplicate it.

---

## 6. Canonical Reference Principle

One subject has:

- one Canonical meaning;
- one Canonical ID;
- one Canonical Source;
- one Canonical Path.

All other appearances are references.

This principle is intended to prevent duplication, conflicting versions, and fragmented authority.

---

## 7. Classification Decision Sequence

For every new subject, the classification process should determine:

1. What is the subject's primary meaning?
2. Which of the ten chapters owns that meaning?
3. What is the correct parent node?
4. Does a Canonical Source already exist?
5. Is the new item a Canonical Source or only a reference?
6. Does the proposed path create duplication or semantic conflict?
7. Is a Human Governance decision required?

---

## 8. Expected Architectural Properties

If the framework and its operating rules are applied correctly, the architecture is expected to provide:

- comprehensive coverage;
- no unnecessary top-level chapters;
- reduced duplication;
- one Canonical Source per subject;
- unlimited recursive expansion;
- stable long-term navigation;
- consistent classification for people and AI;
- compatibility with Canonical First;
- compatibility with Reference First;
- compatibility with Evidence First.

The statement **“không thiếu, không thừa, không trùng lặp”** is an architectural objective. Independent Review must determine whether the proposed rules are sufficient to make this property enforceable.

---

## 9. Risks Requiring Independent Review

Reviewers should examine at least the following risks:

- ambiguity when a subject fits multiple chapters;
- inconsistent Primary Chapter assignment;
- excessive recursion or difficult navigation;
- mixing domain classification with geographic classification;
- unclear distinction between Chapter 0 Foundation and governance content within other chapters;
- duplicate Canonical Sources created through cross-chapter expansion;
- classification changes over time;
- compatibility between dotted presentation paths and numeric Canonical IDs;
- whether the ten chapters truly cover all edge cases without forced classification.

---

## 10. Independent Review Questions

Reviewers are requested to answer:

1. Do Chapters 0–9 provide sufficient top-level coverage for all global fields?
2. Is there any field that cannot reasonably be classified within one of the ten chapters?
3. Are any chapter boundaries materially overlapping or ambiguous?
4. Is the Recursive Expansion Rule logically consistent at every level?
5. Does the Primary Chapter Principle sufficiently prevent duplicate Canonical Sources?
6. Does the Canonical Reference Principle provide a workable method for cross-chapter subjects?
7. Is there any conflict between Universal Coverage and Recursive Expansion?
8. What classification edge cases require explicit rules?
9. What validation rules are needed for machine enforcement?
10. Is this architecture suitable to proceed toward Foundation Architecture status?

---

## 11. Required Review Format

Each Independent Review should distinguish clearly between:

- **Verified Findings**
- **Evidence Gaps**
- **Architecture Risks**
- **Recommendations**
- **Required Changes**
- **Conclusion:** PASS, PASS WITH CHANGES, FAIL, or REVIEW DEFERRED — EVIDENCE GAP

Reviewers must not introduce new CFP+ rules without labeling them as proposals.
